About

What is it like working with Yemi?

Curious, direct, and a little obsessed with the gap between what a product does and what it feels like to use.

My Story

I didn't set out to become a product designer. I set out to figure out why some products felt effortless and others felt like arguing with a machine.

That question took me through six years of fintech, enterprise software, community platforms and a few emerging-tech detours that didn't always ship, but always taught me something. Along the way I learned that most of what separates a good product from a forgettable one isn't cleverness — it's the discipline to keep asking what a person actually needs, even after the roadmap says you already know.

Somewhere in the middle of that, I started teaching. Explaining a decision to a room of designers turned out to be the fastest way to find out whether the decision was actually any good. I've been doing both — building and teaching — ever since, and each one keeps the other honest.

How I Work

A few things that stay true across every project, regardless of industry.

01

Start with the real behavior, not the requested feature

What people ask for and what they need are often different things. I spend early time understanding the gap before committing to a direction.

02

Design decisions should survive being explained out loud

If I can't explain why a screen looks the way it does in one sentence, it usually means I haven't finished thinking yet.

03

Write before you design

A flow that doesn't read clearly as a sentence rarely reads clearly as a screen. Copy is a design tool, not a finishing touch.

04

Collaboration beats polish

The best work I've shipped came from tight loops with engineers and founders, not from disappearing to perfect a file alone.
