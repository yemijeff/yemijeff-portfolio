Books worth reading

A running list of what’s actually shaped how I think about products, not just what looked good on a shelf.

People ask for book recommendations more than almost anything else in portfolio reviews, so here’s the honest, short version — updated occasionally, not exhaustive.

Next in Notebook

Why simplicity is difficult

→
  • The Design of Everyday Things — Don Norman. The book I still recommend first, mostly because it ruins doors for you in the best way.
  • Thinking, Fast and Slow — Daniel Kahneman. Less about design directly, more about why users behave in ways that don’t match what they say in interviews.
  • Shape Up — Basecamp. The clearest writing I’ve found on scoping work so a team can actually finish it, not just start it.
  • On Writing Well — William Zinsser. Not a design book at all, and one of the most useful design books I own — clarity is clarity, on the page or on the screen.
  • Poor Charlie’s Almanack — Charlie Munger. Read for the mental models, stayed for the reminder that most good judgment is just avoiding a short list of predictable mistakes.
