Case Study 04

Receipt App: making a chore feel like a habit worth keeping

Receipt App — expense tracking

Industry

FinTech

Platform

Mobile

Role

Product Design

Timeline

2021

Overview

Receipt App helps freelancers and small business owners keep every receipt without dreading tax season. I designed the mobile experience from a two-person startup's first prototype through to its public launch.

The category was crowded with expense trackers built for accountants. This one needed to be built for the person standing at the counter after buying supplies, deciding in three seconds whether keeping the receipt was worth the effort.

The Challenge

Design a capture flow fast enough to survive that three-second decision window, and a review experience that didn't feel like unpaid bookkeeping homework once a month.

Context

I joined pre-launch, working from user interviews the founders had already conducted with freelance tradespeople and small shop owners who had all, independently, described the same shoebox of paper receipts they meant to deal with "eventually."

Understanding the Users

I interviewed 15 freelancers and small business owners about their current receipt habits, and watched several handle a real purchase in person.

  • Most people who intended to track receipts abandoned the habit within two weeks of trying a new tool.
  • The dropout point was almost always categorization — deciding what a receipt was "for" felt like real work.
  • People were far more willing to snap a photo than to fill in any field at the moment of purchase.
I'll always remember to take the photo. I will never remember to go back and organize it later. — Freelance electrician

Research

We tracked how long each step of a typical competitor's capture flow took. Categorization alone accounted for nearly half the total time, and it was the step most often abandoned mid-flow.

Research Finding
Capture-time habits were durable; organize-later habits were not. Anything we deferred to "later" had a high chance of never happening at all.

Problem Definition

The app needed to move all the effort of organizing into the moment of capture, using automation instead of asking the user to categorize by hand — or accept that most categorization would need to happen without the user at all.

Opportunity Statement

How might we let someone build a complete, tax-ready receipt record by doing nothing more than what they were already willing to do — take a photo?

Exploration

We explored manual categorization with smart defaults, a fully automated OCR-based category suggestion users could correct later, and a hybrid where the app made a confident guess shown as a small, dismissible tag rather than a form field. The dismissible tag pattern won — it never blocked the capture flow and still let corrections happen in idle moments, like waiting for coffee.

User Flows

The entire capture flow was reduced to two taps: open camera, snap photo. Everything else — vendor detection, amount extraction, category suggestion — happened automatically afterward, visible but never blocking.

User flow — two-tap capture with background processing

Wireframes

Early wireframes tested a confirmation screen after every capture against no confirmation screen at all. Removing the confirmation screen entirely — trusting the automation and letting users correct later — increased completed captures significantly in testing.

Low-fidelity wireframes — capture flow exploration

Design Decisions

  • No form at the moment of capture. The camera shutter is the only required interaction; everything else happens after the fact.
  • Corrections as light touches, not chores. Suggested categories appear as small tags that can be changed with a single tap, never as a required field.
  • A monthly review that feels like a summary, not an audit. The end-of-month view was framed around a short, readable story of spending rather than a spreadsheet-style table.

High Fidelity Designs

The visual system used a warmer, more approachable palette than typical finance tools, reinforcing that this was meant to feel like a habit, not an obligation.

High-fidelity UI — capture flow & monthly summary

The two-tap capture flow and end-of-month spending summary, from the launched app.

Testing

A six-week beta with 40 users showed the two-tap flow held up: most participants were still actively capturing receipts at the end of the beta, well past the two-week point where prior tools had seen mass abandonment.

Reflection

This project made the case, clearly, that the "later" step in any workflow is the riskiest step in the whole product. If a habit needs to survive contact with a busy person's day, as much decision-making as possible has to happen in the moment they're already motivated to act.

Lessons Learned

  • Deferred organization is a habit killer; front-loading automation protects the behavior you actually need.
  • Removing a confirmation step can be a bigger win than improving it.
  • A finance tool doesn't have to look or feel like one to be trusted with financial data.
